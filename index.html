1099 Readiness Check

I help small-business owners get ready to file 1099s for subcontractors and other payees. I will review your payee list against official IRS rules and give you a printable report and a W-9 request list. I will not file for you, give tax advice, or ask for full Social Security or employer identification numbers. You can tell me whether you have a TIN, or only the last four digits.

Limits

  • This is not tax advice or a filing service. This page will not file, pay, or contact the IRS or a state agency for you.
  • On a close call, the result is flagged for a CPA or enrolled agent. It is not a final verdict.
  • Do not enter a full Social Security number, full EIN, bank or card number, PIN, password, or login. For a TIN, choose “have it,” “missing,” or enter only the last four digits.
  • If a full TIN is pasted, it is removed and is not stored.
  • The list stays in this browser. Confirm every dollar figure and deadline on the official page before you rely on it.
  • Report a 1099 only for payments made in your trade or business. Personal payments are not reportable.
1. Tax year

Which tax year are we checking? That is the calendar year you made the payments. The dollar threshold is different for 2025 and 2026.

2. Business

Use this only for payments made in the course of that trade or business.

3. North Carolina

Are this business or any payees in North Carolina? This adds the state note. It does not change the federal result.

4. Payees

Paste or upload the list of payees and amounts. A CSV file, or cells pasted from a spreadsheet, is fine. If you use Excel, save the sheet as CSV, or paste the cells here. Do not include full Social Security or EIN numbers.

How were these amounts paid? Mark credit card, debit card, PayPal, and other payment-app rows. Those are often reported on Form 1099-K by the processor, not by you. Add the entity type if you know it. If you have a W-9, say so without sending the TIN.
